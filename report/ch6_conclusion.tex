\chapter{Discussion and Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{What the comparison found}

The four-variant design was built to answer the question ``which judge is
best?'' and it answered a prior question instead: \textbf{the score weights
matter far more than the choice of judge}. Learning five interpretable
weights from the corpus moved the median rank of the correct arrangement,
among roughly four thousand candidates, from about 2,395 to nine.
Substituting one judge for another moved it by one or two places. Any
future effort on this pipeline is better spent on the ordering function
than on the classifier that adjusts it.

None of the three judges repays its cost end to end: all three move the
ranking by one or two places in mixed directions, and none separates from
the baseline at conventional significance. As classifiers in isolation,
though, the image judge is the strongest of the three (0.995 ROC AUC), and
the blanked-wall control establishes that its advantage there is a matter
of information rather than architecture: the convolutional network does
not read column patterns better than a gradient-boosted model reads
measurements of them; it wins because a picture can carry the relationship
between columns and walls across sources drawn in incompatible units,
which six scale-independent measurements cannot.

The quantum result is the cleanest negative finding in the work. A
four-qubit circuit learns this task to the level of a linear model on the
same four measurements, and behaves like one when given more data: fifty
times the training set moves it by 0.007, while equally informed tree
ensembles gain 0.075. Its ceiling is expressive capacity, not data volume.
That is a sharper statement than a single small-sample score can support,
and it required the size sweep and the fixed test set to obtain.

\section{The signal that dominates, and its circularity}

One signal recurs throughout the results: whether columns coincide with
load-bearing walls. It appears as the largest learned score weight, as the
image judge's 0.995 with the wall layer present against 0.874 without, and
as the reason the image judge is the only one that helps end to end.

The qualification must be stated as often as the finding. The derived
labels place columns inside load-bearing walls \emph{by definition}, so the
strength of that signal is partly a restatement of the labelling
procedure rather than a discovery about engineering practice. What the
results establish is that \emph{on this corpus, with these labels}, the
strongest available indication that an arrangement is real is its
registration with the walls, and that every representation permitted to see
the walls finds it. Whether that also holds for arrangements a practising
engineer would draw is not established here, and cannot be without the
external check that Section~\ref{sec:further} names.

\section{Negative results, and why they are reported}

Three results in this thesis are negative, and each is reported as an
outcome.

No variant beats the baseline at conventional significance on seven plans;
every $p$-value is 1.000. The equal-weight score is actively wrong about
density for wall-bearing buildings, to the point where deleting the
density measure improves its accuracy fourfold. And the quantum arm,
trained on the same 80\% partition as the other judges, recovers the
correct arrangement first on one of the seven plans -- the same plan the
baseline and the image judge also recover -- and is otherwise the weakest
of the four.

These are reported because the protocol was designed to make them
reportable. The baseline was retained rather than discarded once the
learned variants were built; comparisons are paired rather than presented
as competing averages; the sample size and its consequences are stated
rather than left implicit; and candidate coverage is reported before any
accuracy figure so that no result can be inflated by a ceiling the reader
cannot see. A protocol that can only produce favourable findings is not a
protocol.

\section{What did not work}

Several approaches were tried and abandoned, and the record is useful.

The cost function was removed for the reason given in
Section~\ref{sec:removals}: its unit rates could not be defended, and no
improvement to the member sizing beneath them would have changed that. Two
sizing errors inside it, sizing beams on bending capacity alone and
neglecting column buckling, were corrected before the whole apparatus was
deleted; the corrections are recorded in the repository history rather than
here, since the stage no longer exists.

The circular training examples described in Section~\ref{sec:negatives}
inflated the feature judge by roughly a tenth of its reported score. An
earlier conclusion that hand-made features beat a convolutional network on
this data has been superseded: that experiment showed the network grid
lines only, with no wall layer and with columns placed at every crossing,
under the earlier labels. On grid lines alone the measurements did win; on
arrangements shown in their architectural context the pictures do.

Two errors were made and caught during this revision, and both are
instructive. The blend that combines score with judge initially
standardised the penalty within the shortlist rather than across the
candidate set, which muted the judge entirely; it was caught because all
four variants returned byte-identical tables. And the first attempt at the
quantum size sweep used different test sets at different scales, which made
the circuits appear to degrade with more data; it was caught because the
capacity-matched control degraded by the same amount.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The labels are derived, not observed.} They are an
equivalent-frame idealisation of wall-bearing buildings, conditioned on a
160\,mm threshold. The derivation is documented, externally sanity-checked
against an independent building inventory, and its constant is varied with
the effect reported. It remains a modelling decision, and every accuracy
figure in this work means agreement with it.

\item \textbf{No measurement is independent of the labels.} An earlier
version of this work included a lateral drift index, which provided a
second basis of comparison that did not depend on the labelling procedure.
It has been removed. If the procedure is systematically wrong, no result
reported here would reveal it. The threshold sensitivity bounds the effect
of one assumption \emph{within} the procedure; it cannot test the
procedure itself.

\item \textbf{One signal is partly circular}, as
Section~\ref{sec:mlresults} and the discussion above state at each
appearance.

\item \textbf{Seven evaluation plans are few.} Paired tests are used and
their limited power is reported. The protocol scales without modification
to a larger held-out set, and enlarging it is straightforward.

\item \textbf{The image judge discards absolute scale by construction.} It
cannot distinguish a six-metre span from a six-foot one. This is a
deliberate trade for cross-source comparability, and the blanked-wall
control quantifies what the trade buys.

\item \textbf{The quantum comparison uses one split per training size},
so the small movements between sizes cannot be separated from run-to-run
variation. The sweep is read for its trend, not for its individual
figures.

\item \textbf{Class balance is synthetic.} The judges are trained and
tested at equal class proportions by construction, so neither ROC AUC nor
PR AUC estimates performance at deployment prevalence.

\item \textbf{CubiCasa's labels rest on a proportional rather than a
physical threshold}, because its drawings have no recoverable scale.

\item \textbf{Enumeration beyond the configured limit is sampled}, so
completeness is not guaranteed for heavily subdivided plans.

\item \textbf{Angled walls are excluded from the evaluation}, because the
labelling procedure cannot represent a column standing off the rectangular
grid. The angled-wall path exists in the pipeline and is exercised only in
demonstration.

\item \textbf{The corpus is residential and the target is commercial.}
Swiss Dwellings contains apartment blocks of 12 to 37\,m across; the frames
this pipeline is ultimately aimed at are larger and more regular.

\item \textbf{The novelty claim rests partly on absence of evidence.}
Searches found no comparable study, but no search establishes that none
exists; the claim is worded to be falsifiable by a single
counter-example.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Candidate coverage is a design property, not a given.} The
correct arrangement appears among the candidates only because the parser
and the generator were aligned with the labelling procedure. Coverage must
be reported before accuracy, or the accuracy of several methods will
silently measure one shared ceiling.

\item \textbf{Five interpretable weights, learned by ranking, outperform
both hand-set equality and an opaque second model.} The median rank of the
correct arrangement improves from about 2,395 to nine. For wall-bearing
residential stock the designerly priors on density and regularity point in
the wrong direction, which a learned linear ranker exposes and a black box
would have concealed.

\item \textbf{Judges are second-order once the ordering function is
right}, and only the appearance-based judge improves on the score alone,
consistently in direction though not significantly at this sample size.

\item \textbf{A four-qubit circuit learns the task to matched-classical
level and no further, and more data does not move it.} Its ceiling is
expressive capacity. No advantage is claimed in either direction, and the
computational cost of establishing even that much was four orders of
magnitude greater than the classical control's.

\item \textbf{The strongest plausibility signal available on this corpus
is column-wall registration}, and a thesis built on derived labels must
say, as this one does, that the signal is partly its own derivation
reflected back.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Further work}
\label{sec:further}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An expert rating study.} Ten to twenty plans, each variant's
first choice presented alongside the derived label, rated blind by a
practising structural engineer. This is the highest-value addition
available, because it would convert every claim in this report from
``the model agrees with our labelling procedure'' into ``the model agrees
with a human engineer''.

\item \textbf{Reinstate a measurement independent of the labels.} Any
check that does not depend on the derived arrangement would restore what
the removal of the drift axis took away. A lateral or gravity calculation
with real member sizes would serve, as would the study above.

\item \textbf{Recover absolute scale for CubiCasa5K}, most plausibly from
standard door widths, so that the physical threshold applies to a second
source and cross-source claims rest on two sources rather than one.

\item \textbf{Enlarge the held-out set} until the paired tests have the
power to resolve the differences the present tables only suggest.

\item \textbf{Learn the placement rule itself.} The generator currently
chooses between two fixed rules, lattice and walls. Which crossings of a
chosen grid receive a column is itself a learnable decision, and it is
the part of the problem this work has left most untouched.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reproduction}

Every notebook in the repository reads committed data tables and runs
without the raw archives. The corpus, the labels, the load-bearing walls
and every results table are published, which is sufficient to retrain all
four learned components. The following reproduces the results in this
report:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
python 02\_data\_pipeline/regenerate\_corpus.py --swiss ... --cubicasa ...\\
python 07\_layout\_classifier/train\_ml1.py\\
python 08\_ml\_ranker/train\_learned\_weights.py\\
python 10\_quantum\_ml/train\_quantum.py\\
python 11\_cnn\_baseline/train\_ml2.py\\
python 11\_cnn\_baseline/train\_ml2\_ablation.py\\
python 12\_evaluation/evaluate.py --variants V1,V2,V3,V4
\end{quote}

The first command requires the two raw archives, which are large and are
not committed; it validates its output against the committed tables before
overwriting them. The remaining commands require only the repository.
